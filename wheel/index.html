Sell the put.
Get the stock. Sell the call.

A weekly wheel on Amazon. Every Monday I sell a cash-secured put about 5% below the stock. If I get assigned, I sell covered calls at or above my cost until the shares get called away, then start over. This page has every trade, the account every day, and whether it beat just holding the stock.

01 / THESIS AND RESULT

Did it beat holding the stock?

My thesis before running it: Amazon's weekly options have a lot of premium in them relative to how far the stock usually moves in a week, so selling a 5%-out-of-the-money put every week should pay me most weeks and only hand me the stock after a drop, which is a price I would have been happy to pay anyway. Covered calls at or above my cost should then add income while I wait to get out. I expected the wheel to give up some upside if the stock ran, but to have a smaller drawdown and a better Sharpe ratio than buy and hold.

02 / THE ALGORITHM

One rule per step, decided before the run.

The loop only looks at prices that existed at the decision time. Nothing uses a later price to pick a strike or to undo a trade.

01 / MONDAY, FLAT

Sell a cash-secured put

On the first trading day of the week, if I hold no shares and no option, I pick the highest strike at or below 95% of that day's close, expiring that Friday. I need a real two-sided quote with a bid of at least $0.05 and a spread under half the mid. If that strike has no usable quote I step down one strike, twice at most, then skip the week.

Size: as many contracts as the cash can fully cover (strike × 100 each), capped at 2. Fee $0.65 per contract.

02 / FRIDAY, EXPIRY

Settle at the close

If the stock closes below the strike I am assigned: I buy 100 shares per contract at the strike and cash goes down by strike × 100. If it closes at or above the strike the put expires and I keep the premium. Weeks where the Friday is a holiday settle on the Thursday the option actually expired.

03 / MONDAY, LONG STOCK

Sell a covered call

Holding shares, I sell one call per 100 shares at the lowest strike that is at least the higher of the close and my assignment strike. Same quote rules; step up one strike, twice at most. If nothing at or above my cost is worth at least $0.05 I just hold the shares that week and write it down as a skipped week.

04 / FRIDAY, CALL EXPIRY

Called away or keep going

Close above the call strike: shares are delivered at the strike, I am flat, and the next Monday I start at step 1. Close at or below: the call expires, I keep the shares and the premium, and go back to step 3. No rolling and no buying back, so every option runs to its expiry.

WHY THESE CHOICES

The small decisions matter more than the big idea

5% out of the money is roughly a 25–30 delta put on a stock with Amazon's weekly volatility: far enough that most weeks expire, close enough that the premium is worth having. Calls at or above my cost is the classic wheel discipline. It stops me from locking in a loss on the shares, at the price of some weeks with no income when the stock is well below my basis. The Fills section runs the other version too.

WHAT I DID NOT DO

No rolls, no early exits, no leverage

I did not roll puts that went in the money and I did not buy back cheap calls early. Both are common in practice, but each one is another rule to justify, and the assignment is about getting the base loop right and reporting it exactly. Every trade is filled at the quoted bid, which is the worse side of the spread for a seller. Cash is never negative and the put is always fully covered.

Where the numbers come from

Or locally with Workspace open: LSEG_CONFIG=lseg-data.config.json python wheel_fetch.py. The app key stays in that config file, never in the repo.

2. Run

The engine is one Python file. It reads the source JSON, walks the days in order, and writes the book, blotter, ledger, decision log and the stress variants.

python engine.py --source amzn
