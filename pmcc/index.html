02 / THE ALGORITHM

One position. Explicit exit rules.

One AMZN long call, at most one short call, no leverage through position scaling. Decisions use observations available at that time. Rule design benefited from knowledge of this sample’s data gaps, so model-selection hindsight remains.

01 / LONG EXPOSURE

Buy a deep ITM call

On April 6 at 11:00 New York time, select the January 21, 2028 call closest to 70% of spot, within 60–80%, on a $5 strike grid. Break ties toward the lower strike. Buy one at ask. If this opening quote is unavailable, do not move the start date.

Moneyness is a reproducible delta proxy, not a claim that delta equals 0.80. Valid long-call spread: at most 10% of mid.

02 / SELL INCOME

Write the weekly call

Monday 11:00: select the lowest valid $5 strike at or above 103% of spot among the first three candidates, above the long strike. Sell one Friday-expiry call at bid. The short remains backed by the later-expiring call.

Both bid and ask must be positive, uncrossed, and observed in the same labeled bar. Short spread: at most 50% of mid. Skip a missing entry.

03 / CLOSE THE RISK

Buy the short back

After entry, check each hourly observation from 10:00 through 16:00 New York time. Buy back at ask when ASK ≤ 50% of the original sale BID, before fees. Otherwise close Friday at 15:00, or sooner at a daily 15:00 check if ITM with mid extrinsic value ≤ $0.05. Do not re-enter that week.

Skip weeks with a Monday or Friday market holiday. A missing mandatory exit halts the simulation. The take-profit trigger never substitutes a stale quote or a trade print for an ask.

Final liquidation rule: October 2 at 15:00 New York time. Close any remaining short first, then sell the long call and stock benchmark positions at bid. Halt if a required liquidation quote is unavailable.

Exit counts are calculated from the booked trades below.

THE CRITICAL DIFFERENCE

The short call is not stock-covered.

Early assignment creates short stock against the long call. The long call does not exercise itself merely because the short was assigned. Exercising it voluntarily can destroy remaining time value.

A conditional base case

The base path assumes no external assignment before a profit-target, risk-triggered, or scheduled buyback. Monitoring reduces exposure; it cannot rule out overnight or intraday assignment. Amazon’s no-cash-dividend history removes one common trigger, not all assignment risk.

Assignment and worse-fill scenarios will be reported with the real run. They are scenarios, not assigned probabilities.

Stress the conclusion

Separate alternative assumptions from the base path. No simulated stress price is presented as an observed fill.
