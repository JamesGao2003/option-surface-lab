01 / EQUITY & CAPITAL REQUIREMENTS

The life of the account

NAV includes the negative value of the short call. Initial margin = 50% of stock market value; maintenance = 25%. Missing marks produce gaps, never invented valuations.

Available funds = NAV − initial margin. Excess equity = NAV − maintenance. The dashed zero line is a funding boundary, not a profit target.

Every written call uses a simulated limit fill at the contemporaneous hourly midpoint, (BID + ASK) / 2. We require a positive, non-crossed two-sided quote.

05 / WHAT HAPPENED, AND WHAT TO CHANGE

The result needs a reason

What the account did

Awaiting a real-data account.

Where the assumption meets reality

Quote availability determines which weeks can be traded. Missing observations never become fills.

The July 27 entry bought AAPL at $338.21; it closed July 31 at $309.05. The $602.50 call premium cushioned only part of that $2,916 stock decline, leaving a $2,313.50 loss from the entry mark to expiry. The unfilled August 3 call then left the retained shares uncapped and unhedged by a new premium.

Because upside above the strike is delivered to the call holder, collected premium must be assessed alongside foregone stock gains and the remaining downside exposure.

What I would change next

First, resolve the failed expiry groups and repeat the same predeclared rules; those missing weeks may materially change the outcome. Then replace guaranteed midpoint fills with a bid-side or queue-aware execution model and include explicit fees. Next, measure quote age and trade size, and compare alternative strike distances out of sample. Do not tune the strike after looking at this window's return.

Before treating this as an investable strategy, include dividends, ex-dividend early exercise, corporate actions, borrowing costs and broker margin rules. This assignment is a price-only, expiry-only simulation.

Algorithm, accounting equations & modelling decisions

Entry and strike selection

Use one name, AAPL.O, and one contract per week. Decide at Monday 11:00 America/New_York using hourly bars explicitly labelled at period end. This avoids the open's incomplete first hour and avoids interpreting a bar-start label as an already known price.

Construct a $2.50 grid from this Monday's observed stock print, including a few ITM levels for diagnostics and OTM strikes no more than $15 above spot. Among Friday calls on that declared grid with positive BID ≤ ASK at the exact decision timestamp, select the lowest strike ≥ spot. This is nearest quoted OTM on the sampled grid, not a claim of an exhaustive historical chain.

When no usable stock print or call quote exists at that time, skip the week with no later entry. A holiday Monday is skipped. No stock-only entry is booked when the option leg fails. If shares are already held, do not buy another 100. Check Reg T capital before either new leg is booked.

Expiry and valuation

Hold to Friday 16:00 New York. If spot > strike, record call ASSIGN and stock SELL at strike, leaving zero shares and zero calls. Otherwise record EXPIRE and retain the shares; exact ATM is assumed unexercised. No rolls, buy-to-close orders, discretionary exits or early assignment.

Require a usable expiry stock print. If it is absent, stop the backtest and report unresolved settlement rather than invent a Friday close. Midpoint gaps during the holding period create gaps in marked NAV. At maturity the option is marked at intrinsic value immediately before physical settlement.

The stock print and option quote refer to the same hourly end label, not necessarily the same tick. Execution at those bar observations is a stated simulation convention; prices are not asserted to be simultaneous executable ticks.

Reg T, under the assignment model

NAV = cash + stock MV + option MV
LMV = shares × stock mark
Short call MV = −100 × call mark
Initial = 0.50 × LMV
Maintenance = 0.25 × LMV
Available = NAV − Initial
Excess = NAV − Maintenance

A fully covered call adds no naked-option charge in this model. Reg T is used because its prescribed rules can be reproduced here without a broker-specific portfolio-margin engine. These are the assignment's simplified requirements, not a claim about every broker's house rules.

Initial cash is $30,000. No fees, interest, dividends, taxes or share adjustments enter the base-case cash account. The benchmark holds 100 AAPL shares from the first actual strategy entry with the same remaining cash; it is price-only and not a fully invested benchmark.
